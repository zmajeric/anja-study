Reading a cataract off the Y sutures

The lens grows like an onion, oldest in the middle — so where an opacity sits tells you when it formed.

~20 minSource: Handout 4 · Slides 4 · Textbook ch. 7 (+ ch. 9)Next: the uvea
The one win for today

Shown a slit-lamp picture, find the anterior upright Y and the posterior inverted Y, and say which nucleus an opacity is in — and therefore whether the patient was born with it.

What the exam asks

Only four objectives, and three of them are the same idea: describe how the embryonic nucleus forms · how the lens fibers form around it · how the Y sutures can be used to determine in which nucleus a cataract is located · identify the lens capsule, Y sutures and embryonic nucleus.

Exam guidelines · p. 2

1. How the centre is made

surface ectoderm→lens placode (~day 27)→lens pit→lens vesicle (~day 33)→posterior cells elongate forward = primary fibres→embryonic nucleus

Three consequences fall straight out of this, and all three are examinable:

FactBecause
The adult lens has no posterior epitheliumit was used up making the primary fibres
The embryonic nucleus has no suturesthe primary fibres simply fill the vesicle back to front; they meet nothing
The capsule is there from the startit is the vesicle's own basal lamina, visible by 5 weeks

TB 7 · p. 97–98 · TB 9 · p. 140–145

Follow A to E and say out loud what happens at each step. In E the lumen has gone: that filled core is the embryonic nucleus, and every fibre made for the rest of your life is laid down outside it.

Note where the cells point: apical surfaces face the lumen, basal surfaces face out. That is why, later, the anterior suture is made of apical ends and the posterior suture of basal ends.

2. How every later fibre is added

cell divides in the germinative zone→daughter moves to the equator→elongates: apex forward, base backward→meets fibres from the other side = a suture→loses its organelles

The germinative zone is the band of epithelium just in front of the equator, and it keeps dividing for life. Because this happens all the way round, each new layer is a complete shell — your own note put it best: "like an onion". The oldest fibres are in the middle; the youngest are at the surface.

TB 7 · p. 98, 104

3. The four zones, and what each one means

Zone (centre → out)Fibres madeSutures
Embryonic nucleusthe primary fibres, from the posterior epitheliumnone
Fetal nucleussecondary fibres formed before birththe Y sutures: upright in front, inverted behind
Adult nucleusbirth → sexual maturationmore stellate, branching
Cortexafter sexual maturationcomplex stars, 9–15 branches by old age
The rule the objective is really asking for

Find the anterior upright Y and the posterior inverted Y. Then:

Between the two Ys, in the centre → embryonic nucleus — it formed before the sutures existed, so the cause is developmental.

On or along a Y → fetal nucleus — the Y sutures belong to it. Also present at birth.

In front of the anterior Y or behind the posterior Y → adult nucleus or cortex — a later shell. Spokes mean cortical.

Which suture is this, and which zone does it lie in?

Answer

The posterior Y suture — inverted means posterior — lying in the posterior part of the fetal nucleus. An opacity sitting exactly on it would be a sutural cataract, formed before birth.

4. The capsule, in five lines

A basement membrane — with time the thickest in the body — mostly collagen, with no elastic fibres yet highly elastic thanks to its lamellar build. It is semipermeable: it keeps large molecules out and lets nutrients in, which gives the lens its immune privilege. Thickest in the ring around the anterior pole (it keeps being added to by the anterior epithelium), thinnest at the posterior pole, about 3.5 µm. In cataract surgery the anterior capsule is opened, the contents removed, and the rest left in place to hold the implant.

TB 7 · p. 97–98, 108

5. Three cataracts worth telling apart

TypeWhereThe clinical giveaway
Nucleara nucleusyellow to brown (brunescence); raises lens power, so a myopic shift — a hyperope may briefly see better
Corticalthe cortexspokes following the fibres, thick peripherally; hurts vision only once it reaches the pupil
Posterior subcapsularjust under the posterior capsulehurts vision early — it sits on the visual axis; linked to long-term high-dose steroids and radiation

Numbers worth owning

Power ≈ 20 D unaccommodated · accommodation 14 D at age 8–12 → near zero after 50 · thickness 3.6–4.5 mm, growing 0.02 mm a year · diameter 6.5 mm infant → 9.0 × 9.7 mm adult · refractive index 1.38 cortex, 1.41 nucleus · capsule 11–15 µm anterior pole, ~7 µm equator, ~3.5 µm posterior pole · Y sutures visible in month 3.
