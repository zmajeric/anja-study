Secondary Dermatological Descriptors

• Sessile: A lesion fixed to the skin on a broad base • Pedunculated: A lesion on a stalk • Papillomatous: A lesion exhibiting a surface resembling a cauliflower or artichoke • Scales: Shedding, dead epidermal cells, dry or greasy • Umbilicated: The lesion exhibits a central crater like an umbilicus or belly button • Crusts: Dried masses of skin exudates • Ulcer: Irregularly sized and shaped excavations extending into the dermis

Uncontrolled cell growth

1. Neoplasia – Mass of new cells which proliferate without control and serve no useful function 2. Anaplasia – loses resemblance to cell of origin – AKA tumor or cancer

7

Tumors can be divided into two general classes:

benign & malignant

• A malignant tumor can metastasize into lymph nodes & distant organs • A malignant tumor, if untreated, may kill the patient wherever it occurs. Usually a benign tumor may only cause death if it happens to grow in a vital organ. • A malignant tumor infiltrates the surrounding tissue. A benign tumor grows by expansion and is usually separated from the surrounding tissue by a capsule. • When a malignant tumor is excised, it may recur. When a benign tumor is removed, it usually does not recur. • Generally, a malignant tumor grows more rapidly than a benign tumor. 8

Nomenclature: by parenchymal cell of origin

1. Carcinoma – Epithelial tissue 2. Sarcoma – Tumor of CT (derived from mesoderm), which develops into cartilage and bone (choristoma), fat (lipoma), and muscle (myoma) 3. Hemangioma – Tumor comprised of blood vessels/vasculature 4. Lymphoma – Tumor of lymphatic tissue (leukemia – hematopoietic cells affected) 5. Melanoma – Tumor of pigmented cells 6. Hamartoma – Tumor made of tissue normally present there 7. Blastoma – Tumor derived from embryonic cells (i.e. retinoblastoma) 9

Neoplastic considerations - Subjective

• Lesion does not act or respond as anticipated • HX of other skin lesions elsewhere on the body • HX of other neoplasias or systemic disease • Pts. with HX of excessive UV skin exposure • Lesion is not common for patient’s age, sex, race, demography • Older patients • FHX of skin cancers • Fair-complexion pts. • Acute vs. chronic onset & duration • Rapid or irregular growth patterns • Pain or irritation associated with suspicious lesion

11

Neoplastic considerations - Objective

• Quality of the tissue looks irregular • Bleeding or ulceration of lesion • Surface integrity & quality questionable • Changes in lesion not consistent or predictable • Neovascular patterns around or within lesion • Recurrent infections or inflammations at site • Uncharacteristically large lumps or bumps • Associated tearing or conjunctival hyperemia • Erosion of the margins or surface of a lesion

Malignant Melanoma Mortality via

Clark’s levels and Breslow depth

Clark’s level – less accurate • Level I: epidermis; called "in situ" melanoma; 100% cure • Level II: invasion of the papillary (upper) dermis • Level III: filling papillary dermis, not reticular (lower) dermis • Level IV: invasion of reticular dermis fi~50% dead in 10 yr • Level V: invasion of the deep, subcutaneous tissue

Breslow depth – more accurate • 0.75 mm deep fi0% metastases • 0.85 mm deep fi97% survival in 10 years • 3.6 mm deep fi50% dead in 10 years
