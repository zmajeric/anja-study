Self-check

Which tear layer do the meibomian glands make, and which skin condition goes with meibomianitis? Which way does the link run?
Lipid (oil, sebum). Rosacea: a rosacea patient very likely has meibomianitis; most people with meibomianitis do not have rosacea. Guidelines · p. 2 · Audio · 11:35–12:12
Walk through MGD from gland metabolism to inflammation.
Altered metabolism → thick secretions block the opening → stagnant material feeds bacteria → bacterial lipase → fatty acids → inflammation. Not an infection at first. Lecture 4 · p. 3
Papule or pustule: which has a white head, and what makes one become the other?
Pustule has the white head (pus, sebaceous matter, debris); a papule becomes a pustule after white blood cells invade. Lecture 4 · p. 7–8
Give the facial rosacea treatment and five triggers. Which food has no effect?
Doxycycline 100 mg bid 4–6 weeks; metronidazole gel 0.75% bid 5–8 weeks. Hot spices, alcohol, heated drinks, chocolate, tomatoes, citrus, sun, wind, exercise, weather, stress, H. pylori, vasodilators. Dairy has no significant effect. Lecture 4 · p. 12–13
What can rosacea do to the cornea, and how fast do you follow it up?
PEE (SPK), keratitis, vascularization, thinning (sight-threatening). Tears/ointments, steroids if severe. Follow-up in days with corneal involvement, weeks without. Lecture 4 · p. 14–19
What is PEE, and how does it differ from a marginal infiltrate?
PEE: pits where epithelial cells are missing; fluorescein pools and glows. Marginal infiltrate: a white dot of white blood cells near the limbus. Audio · 08:38–10:00
Describe the "toothpaste" test and what it shows in MGD.
Forced expression of the glands: thickened secretions come out of some glands, many give none; thickened secretions also appear in the tears. Normal meibum is clear oil. Lecture 4 · p. 24
List the MGD plan from first step to patient education.
Warm compresses; expression from tarsus toward the margin (office and home); doxycycline 100 mg bid 6–10 weeks if severe/not responding (inhibits lipase), taper; manage dry eye; treat overlapping staph/seborrheic blepharitis; dermatology if rosacea; follow-up 1 week then 3–4 weeks; chronic, no cure. Lecture 4 · p. 25–28
LipiFlow vs IPL in one line each.
LipiFlow: heat tarsal side + pressure skin side, 12 min, Korb, FDA 2011. IPL: non-laser light on the skin, heats meibum and telangiectasia, raises lipid layer and non-invasive TBUT. Lecture 4 · p. 29–31
A child with honey-crusted lesions; an elderly man with fever and a sharp-edged red cheek. Diagnosis and drug for each?
Child: impetigo (S. aureus/S. pyogenes) → oral cephalosporin/macrolide/dicloxacillin + topical bacitracin/mupirocin after washing. Man: erysipelas (S. pyogenes) → penicillin G injected or V orally, PCP/dermatology. Lecture 4 · p. 32–37
