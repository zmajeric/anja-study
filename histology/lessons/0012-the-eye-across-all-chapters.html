1. Where the eye comes from

The eye is built from surface ectoderm, neuroectoderm, mesoderm and neural crest — never endoderm. Surface ectoderm → anterior epithelium and the lens. Neuroectoderm → the two layers of the optic cup (retina, RPE, and the iris and ciliary epithelia — which is why the iris dilator is a modified epithelium). Neural crest → most of the anterior segment stroma and the angle (first wave deep to surface ectoderm; second wave anterior stroma and angle) — so neural-crest migration disorders show up in the eye alongside face and peripheral-nerve syndromes.

Two developmental analogies Freddo reuses: ectasia (bulge without uveal tissue: keratoconus) vs staphyloma (bulge with uvea showing blue-grey) = meningocele vs myelomeningocele. And the Meibomian gland forms like every exocrine gland: a rod from the lid-margin epithelium drives down, branches, canalises.

3. The five recurring comparisons

  • Cornea vs sclera: same collagen; regular = transparent, irregular = opaque.
  • Ciliary epithelium vs stratified: bilayer of two simple cuboidal sheets, two basement membranes.
  • Iris dilator vs sphincter: myoepithelium (diabetes-sensitive) vs unitary smooth muscle.
  • Ciliary muscle vs iris sphincter: multiunit (no gap junctions) vs unitary (gap junctions).
  • Retinal axons before vs after the lamina cribrosa: bare (light must pass) vs myelinated (speed; diameter doubles).
