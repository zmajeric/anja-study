Rigid connective tissues

Three cartilages, four bone cells, one osteon — and the numbers that separate cartilage from bone.

~20 minSource: Script, Ch. 8 (Cartilage and Bone)Next: soft connective tissue
The one win for today

Tell the three cartilages apart on a slide, describe an osteon from canal to canaliculus, and know the two "odd one out" facts Freddo likes: fibrocartilage has no perichondrium, and osteoclasts are the only bone cell from the blood lineage.

0. Warm-up — muscle

1. Cartilage

Strong, flexible, semi-rigid; resists compression yet bends. Roles: airway framework (nose, trachea, larynx, bronchi), articular surfaces, the template for endochondral ossification (fetal skeleton except the skull; children's growth plates on X-ray). Avascular → nourished by diffusion → transplantable without rejection, like the cornea.

Cells (chondro-): chondroblasts (young, secreting) → chondrocytes (mature) sitting in lacunae — the classic recognition cue. Matrix: ~10% aggrecan (chondroitin sulfate aggregating with hyaluronan — also in the sclera), ~75% water, collagen subtypes that differ by type. The perichondrium is the dense, more cellular band against the pale matrix through which nourishment arrives.

2. Bone — gross and microscopic

Like cartilage plus calcium phosphates (hydroxyapatite) → hard. Five functions: support, protection, movement, mineral homeostasis (Ca, P), blood cell production. Long bone: epiphyses (articular cartilage) · metaphysis (epiphyseal line) · diaphysis (shaft, medullary cavity); compact bone as the outer shell, spongy/cancellous bone as trabeculae in the ends with red marrow; nutrient artery through the nutrient foramen.

Periosteum: outer fibrous layer (fibroblasts) + inner cambium (osteoblast progenitors); vascularised; nociceptive fibres — the bone matrix itself is not innervated for pain, so fracture pain is periosteal. Endosteum lines the medullary cavity.

The osteon (Haversian system) — describe it in one breath

Concentric lamellae around a central Haversian canal carrying an arteriole, venule and nerve; lacunae between lamellae each hold an osteocyte; canaliculi radiate from every lacuna carrying osteocyte processes to neighbours (gap junctions); Volkmann's (perforating) canals run transversely linking osteons to periosteal and medullary vessels; external circumferential lamellae ring the shaft; perforating fibres anchor the periosteum. Ground section: canals = large dark circles, lacunae = small dark ovals in rings, canaliculi = tiny hairs.

Spongy bone: trabeculae — solid, strongly eosinophilic winding bands — with marrow between (cellular, with round pale spaces where fat dissolved).

4. Growth and remodeling

Endochondral ossification: bone into a pre-existing cartilage model — long bones, most of the skeleton. Intramembranous: bone directly onto fibrous connective tissue, no cartilage — flat skull bones (parietal), mandible, maxilla, clavicles. Growing bone: osteoblasts on the surface (precursors above) secrete osteoid → calcifies → trapped osteoblasts become osteocytes with a process in a canaliculus.

Remodeling continues for life: osteoclasts resorb, osteoblasts replace. Signals: mechanical stress (releases Ca) and hormones.

Clinical notes

Parathyroid hormone stimulates resorption (Ca into blood); calcitonin inhibits it — the skeleton as calcium reservoir. Osteoclasts dissolve bone chemically, not mechanically: carbonic anhydrase → acid under the ruffled border → hydroxyapatite (a calcium phosphate salt) dissolves, precisely and locally.
