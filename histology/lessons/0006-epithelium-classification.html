0. Warm-up — embryology and the cell

1. What an epithelium is for

An epithelium separates two environments while letting them interact in a controlled way: selective secretion, selective absorption, transcellular transport, and picking up chemical or sensory messages. Every epithelial cell is polarised — an apical surface facing the lumen/tear film/air and a basal surface that lays down a basement membrane. Polarity is what makes directional transport possible. Two rules for all epithelia: they always contain sensory nerves (in the cornea the nerves are also trophic) and they are avascular, fed by diffusion.

2. The classification: layers × shape

Layers: simple (one layer on a basement membrane) · stratified/compound (many layers, all from a basal layer) · pseudostratified (one layer of cells of different heights that only looks layered) · transitional (changes between two categories with functional cycles) · myoepithelium (epithelial top, smooth-muscle-like bottom). Shapes: squamous (flat, wider than tall) · cuboidal (box) · columnar (taller than wide).

Two rules for stratified epithelia — memorise exactly
  1. Named for the surface cells, not the deeper ones.
  2. Mitosis only in the basal layer. Mitotic figures higher up = the epithelium is not normal.

And the trap: a genuinely stratified sheet has one shared basement membrane on one side only. Two simple epithelia back-to-back (the ciliary epithelium) each have their own — a bilayer, not stratified.

Clinical notes — the two cuboidal/squamous workhorses of the eye

Corneal endothelium (simple squamous): controls stromal water content; seen en face by specular microscopy as the hexagonal mosaic; loss of pattern or cell number is a warning because the cells are not readily replaced. RPE (simple cuboidal): absorbs stray light with melanin, phagocytoses shed outer-segment discs, runs the visual cycle, and — via tight junctions — forms the outer blood-retinal barrier.

3. Cilia

Motile or sensory projections of the apical surface; the cell is stationary and moves fluid past itself (tracheal mucus, ovum in the Fallopian tube, sperm). Skeleton = the axoneme (microtubule bundle), anchored by the basal body (microtubular triplets) through a transition zone. 9 + 2 = motile · 9 + 0 = non-motile/sensory → the photoreceptor connecting cilium; the whole outer segment is a specialised cilium.

Clinical note — Bardet–Biedl syndrome

An inherited ciliopathy: polydactyly (extra digit by the little finger or baby toe) plus a retinitis-pigmentosa-like degeneration, because the same defective ciliary proteins disturb limb development and disable the connecting cilium. RP-like fundus + extra digits → think ciliopathy.

4. Myoepithelium and the cells that live inside epithelia

Myoepithelial cells: epithelial apically, smooth-muscle-like basally; squeeze secretory units and ducts of sweat, mammary, lacrimal and salivary glands. In the eye, a layer of pigmented myoepithelial cells is the dilator pupillae — the anterior myoepithelium (AME) in front of the posterior pigmented epithelium (PPE) — a modified epithelium, not conventional smooth muscle.
