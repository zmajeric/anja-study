From one cell to three layers — and an eye

Four stages in order, the germ layers and what each becomes, why the neural crest owns the front of the eye, and why endoderm owns none of it.

~20 minSource: Script, Ch. 4 (Embryonic Tissue Types)Next: epithelium classification
The one win for today

Tell the story in order — fertilization → cleavage → blastulation → gastrulation → neurulation — with one defining fact per stage, and answer the two classic questions cold: "which germ layer does not contribute to the eye?" (endoderm) and "meningocele vs myelomeningocele = ectasia vs staphyloma".

0. Warm-up — the cell and vessels

1. Egg, sperm, fertilization

The egg is the largest human cell: plasma membrane → zona pellucida (glycoprotein coat) → corona radiata (follicle cells). It carries essentially all the cytoplasm and organelles the embryo starts with. The sperm is built to deliver DNA: head (nucleus capped by the acrosome, a lysosome-like vesicle of enzymes) · mid piece (centriole + helical sheath of mitochondria) · tail (flagellum around the axial filament) · end piece.

Four steps, fixed order: 1 sperm binds glycoprotein sites in the zona → 2 acrosome reaction digests the zona so membranes can meet → 3 cortical reaction: sperm–egg membrane binding releases cortical granule enzymes → 4 male chromatids delivered.

Two clinical notes worth a question each

Block to polyspermy: the cortical enzymes destroy the sperm-binding part of the zona glycoprotein, so no second sperm can attach. Polyspermy → triploid zygote → not viable.

Mitochondrial inheritance: sperm carry ~75–100 mitochondria, the egg ~a quarter million, so mitochondrial DNA and disease come from the mother. Affected mother → all children; affected father → none. Several mitochondrial disorders have ocular signs.

Eye link — 9 + 2 versus 9 + 0

Motile cilia (sperm tail): nine microtubule doublets + a central pair = 9 + 2, with dynein arms, nexin bridges and radial links so doublets slide and the cilium bends. Non-motile cilia: 9 + 0. The example that matters: the connecting cilium of the photoreceptor — everything made in the inner segment passes through it to the outer segment. It does not beat; it conducts. Photoreceptor order: outer segment → connecting cilium + basal body → inner segment (mitochondria, ER, Golgi) → nucleus → axon → synapse.

Eye link — the classic exam point

The eye is built from surface ectoderm, neuroectoderm, mesoderm and neural crest — but NOT endoderm. And because so much of the anterior segment is neural crest, disorders of neural-crest migration show up in the eye — which is why the eye is so often involved in syndromes of the face and peripheral nerves.

4. When the tube doesn't close

Because closure starts at the midpoint, defects are classified above vs below it. Above → anencephaly (no major part of brain, skull, scalp; incompatible with life). Below → spina bifida: occulta (incomplete arch, nothing herniates, maybe a tuft of hair) · meningocele (dural bulge, no cord) · myelomeningocele (dural bulge with cord — more severe). Compatible with life, with varying disability.

Eye link — the same logic in the eye wall

Ectasia = bulge that does NOT contain uveal tissue — keratoconus. Staphyloma = bulge that DOES contain uveal tissue — scleral thinning with pigmented uvea showing, dark blue-grey. Name the bulge by whether the deeper tissue came along: meningocele : myelomeningocele :: ectasia : staphyloma.

5. Every gland grows backwards

Exocrine glands, liver to Meibomian, follow one plan: start at the epithelial surface the product will reach (so the gland never has to find its target later) → a rod of cells drives down → the rod branches into acini/tubules → the rod canalises into a duct to the surface (lid margin; bile duct to gut). Along the gut tube: foregut (coeliac artery: Rathke's pouch, pharyngeal pouches 1–4, lung bud, oesophagus, stomach, liver, gallbladder, pancreatic buds) · midgut (superior mesenteric: vitelline duct, cecal bud) · hindgut (inferior mesenteric: cloaca, allantois).
