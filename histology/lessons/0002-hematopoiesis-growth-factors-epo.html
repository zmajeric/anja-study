1. One ancestor, two lines

Hematopoiesis is the production of all formed elements. Every blood cell descends from a single multipotential hematopoietic stem cell, also called the hemocytoblast. Two properties matter for the exam: it is multipotential (can become anything in the blood) and it renews itself, so the supply is never exhausted.

It splits into two great lines of descent. Learn the tree as a picture — the script draws it, and questions ask "which of these comes from the lymphoid progenitor?"

LineDirect childrenGrandchildren
Common myeloid progenitormegakaryocyte · erythrocyte · mast cell · myeloblastmegakaryocyte sheds thrombocytes; myeloblast → basophil, neutrophil, eosinophil, monocyte (→ macrophage)
Common lymphoid progenitornatural killer cell (a large granular lymphocyte) · small lymphocytesmall lymphocyte → T lymphocyte or B lymphocyte (→ plasma cell)
Three traps
  • The mast cell is myeloid, a sibling of the myeloblast — not a child of it, and not lymphoid despite living in tissue.
  • The monocyte is a myeloblast child, in the same litter as the three granulocytes.
  • The NK cell is lymphoid but is not a "small lymphocyte" — it branches off separately as a large granular lymphocyte.

2. Where it happens — and where cells finish growing up

The site changes during life, and a cell is not always matured in the organ where it was born.

When / whatWhere
During embryogenesisislands of cells in the yolk sac
Post-partum (after birth)bone marrow
T cell maturationThymus (T = Thymus)
B cell maturationbursa of Fabricius in birds → in humans, the bone marrow

The script's memory aid: T cells mature in the Thymus. The B name comes from the bursa of Fabricius, a bird organ; humans have no bursa, so the bone marrow does the job. A common wrong answer is "B = bone marrow" as the origin of the letter — it isn't, but it happens to give the right human organ.

Differentiation is not random: each branch point is driven by a specific combination of interleukins, colony stimulating factors, TNF-α and TGF-β.

3. Growth factors — learn the signatures, not the list

The script lists five factors per branch. Memorising every combination is a poor use of six days. Notice instead that GM-CSF appears at every myeloid branch point and SCF/IL-3 at nearly all of them — those never discriminate between answers. What discriminates is the factor that appears only once. The script's own summary line picks out four; G-CSF is the fifth worth knowing.

Signature factorDrivesHook
TPO (thrombopoietin)myeloid progenitor → megakaryocyte → thrombocytesT for Thrombocyte
Epo (erythropoietin)myeloid progenitor → erythrocyteE for Erythrocyte
IL-5myeloblast → eosinophilthe only branch with IL-5
M-CSFmyeloblast → monocyteM for Monocyte
G-CSFmyeloblast → neutrophil and basophilG for Granulocyte (but not the eosinophil)

Two more patterns cover the rest:

  • Stem cell → myeloid progenitor: IL-1, IL-3, IL-6, GM-CSF, SCF — the "ordinary" myeloid set.
  • Stem cell → lymphoid progenitor: FLT-3 ligand, TNF-α, TGF-β1, IL-2, IL-7, IL-12, SDF-1 — the odd names (FLT-3, SDF-1, TNF, TGF) all point to lymphoid. IL-7 and IL-2 reappear for the small lymphocyte → T lymphocyte step (with IL-1, IL-4, IL-6).

4. Erythropoietin — a feedback loop in one sentence

EPO is a glycoprotein hormone that acts as a cytokine on red cell precursors in the bone marrow. Three facts are the whole story:

  1. Made by: interstitial fibroblasts of the kidney and peri-sinusoidal cells of the liver.
  2. Triggered by: hypoxia.
  3. Acts on: red cell precursors in the marrow → more erythrocytes → more oxygen delivery → less hypoxia → less EPO.

The script's phrasing is worth keeping: when oxygen delivery falls, the kidney senses it and drives the marrow to make more red cells.

Clinical note — recombinant EPO

Used to treat anemia: after blood loss in inflammatory bowel disease, after chemotherapy, or in renal dialysis. The dialysis case is the one to be able to explain: a diseased kidney can no longer make enough EPO, so the patient is anemic for an endocrine reason, not a hematological one — the marrow is fine, it is simply not being told to work.
