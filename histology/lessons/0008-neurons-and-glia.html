0. Warm-up — junctions and epithelia

1. Grey and white

Grey matter = neuronal cell bodies. White matter = mostly myelinated axons; lipid-rich myelin makes it white. Brain: grey outside (cortex), white inside. Spinal cord: reversed — grey H/butterfly inside, white outside. T1 MRI separates them in the living patient (substantia nigra and periaqueductal grey are grey-matter formations).

2. The neuron

PartFacts
Cell body (soma, perikaryon)large, round, euchromatic nucleus with one prominent nucleolus; abundant basophilic cytoplasm = rough ER = Nissl bodies (Franz Nissl, b. 1860) → a highly active protein factory. All protein-making machinery lives here
Axononly one — or none (retinal amacrine cells); the output; myelinated or unmyelinated
Dendritesmany; the inputs; short (a mm or two); dendritic spines are where synapses land; may be myelinated in long peripheral nerves

"Typical" textbook neurons are simply the biggest: cortical pyramidal cells (apical + basal dendrites), spinal motor neurons, and cerebellar Purkinje cells (huge dendritic tree rather than a long axon). Cytoskeleton in an axon cross-section by EM: neurofilaments = the clear areas; microtubules bundled and irregularly spaced in fuzzy actin-rich material.

3. Myelin — who, where, why it matters

  • CNS: oligodendrocytes — one cell, several processes, several axons. PNS: Schwann cells — one cell, one axon. Both leave nodes of Ranvier; depolarisation happens only there, so myelinated conduction is much faster.
  • Unmyelinated axons are slow but are the majority of peripheral sensory and autonomic fibres.
  • Peripheral nerves regenerate; CNS cells do not — optic nerve damage is permanent.
Eye link — the axon that is both

Retinal ganglion cell axons start unmyelinated, because myelin is opaque and they run along the inner retina in front of the photoreceptors. At the lamina cribrosa they become myelinated. Wrapping each axon doubles the optic nerve diameter: ~1.5 mm at the nerve head → ~3 mm leaving the sclera.

Clinical note — myelinated retinal nerve fibre layer

Congenital myelin in front of the lamina cribrosa. Opaque → an absolute scotoma with the shape of the myelin; macula usually spared; stable over time. If the axons die (e.g. glaucoma) they lose their myelin and the patch regresses.

4. Synapses, transport, motor end plates

Chemical synapse (axon end): vesicles of transmitter, voltage-gated Ca2+ channels trigger release, re-uptake pumps; synaptic cleft; post-synaptic density with receptors (often on a spine). Transmitter can stimulate or inhibit. Electrical synapse: current through low-resistance gap junctions (connexons, open or closed).

Axoplasmic flow: soma → synapse is anterograde (needed because ribosomes and ER are only in the soma); slow system a few mm/day, fast system ~100× faster; retrograde flow returns recycled material.

Motor end plate (neuromuscular junction): motor nerve → skeletal, smooth or cardiac muscle; all muscle cells of one neuron = a motor unit. Axon bundle → branches → synaptic knobs (boutons) on end plates; deeply folded post-synaptic membrane.
