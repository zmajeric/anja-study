# Histology Glossary

The canonical language for this workspace. Every lesson, quiz item and learning record uses
these words in these senses. Where the study script offers two words for one thing, this file
picks one; where the script contradicts itself, this file records the resolution rather than
letting each lesson decide again.

Two rules govern what lands here:

- **Terminology rulings are workspace decisions** and are recorded as soon as the ambiguity is
  met, because lessons need a consistent vocabulary from the first one.
- **Definitions are promoted on evidence.** A term enters the list below once the learner can
  use it correctly, not when it is first taught. Coverage is not understanding.

Not to be confused with [`reference/glossary.html`](./reference/glossary.html), which is the
printable learner-facing sheet covering every term in a chapter. This file is shorter by
design: it holds only the words that needed a decision.

## Conventions

- **Freddo's usage wins.** Where the lecturer's convention differs from a standard textbook,
  teach his, because his is what the exam asks. Flagged per term below.
- **English is the exam language.** Slovenian terms in the script are comprehension aids and
  never the primary name for a concept.
- **Where the script gives two numbers, both are taught.** The overview and detail sections
  disagree on several differential-count ranges; neither is "wrong", and a question may use
  either. See `NOTES.md` for the list.

## Terms

### Chapter 1 — Blood and hematopoiesis

**Formed elements**:
The cellular fraction of blood — erythrocytes, platelets and leukocytes — as opposed to the
fluid matrix they sit in.
_Avoid_: blood cells (a platelet is a fragment, not a cell)

**Eosinophilic**:
Staining pink through uptake of an acidic dye, eosin.
_Avoid_: acidophilic — the script uses both words for this; prefer eosinophilic throughout,
since it names the actual dye

**Basophilic**:
Staining blue or purple through uptake of a basic dye, such as methylene blue.

**Platelet**:
An anucleate cytoplasmic fragment shed from a megakaryocyte, 2–3 µm across.
_Avoid_: thrombocyte — the script gives both; platelet is the exam-facing English

**Band neutrophil**:
A neutrophil whose nucleus is still horseshoe-shaped and unsegmented.
_Avoid_: stab cell, band form

**Left shift**:
A raised proportion of band neutrophils above the normal 3–5%.
_Avoid_: shift to the left

**Diapedesis**:
Passage of a leukocyte out of a vessel through its wall.
_Avoid_: extravasation, migration

**Histiocyte**:
A macrophage residing in connective tissue, derived from a monocyte that has left the blood.
_Avoid_: tissue macrophage — accurate, but histiocyte is Freddo's term and the one an exam
question will use

**Hemocytoblast**:
The self-renewing multipotential stem cell from which every formed element descends.
_Avoid_: multipotential stem cell on its own (too generic once other lineages appear)

## Flagged ambiguities

- **Neutrophil granules.** The chapter overview calls them "blue"; the detail section calls
  them tiny, light-staining and hard to see. The detail section is the recognition cue and the
  one to rely on at the microscope; the overview wording may still appear in a question stem.
- **Epo / EPO.** The script writes "Epo" in the growth-factor list and "EPO" in the
  erythropoietin section. Use **EPO** in prose and quiz stems; "Epo" only when quoting the
  branch-point list, where it sits next to TPO. (Lesson 2 follows this.)
- **Natural killer cell.** Write it out; "NK cell" only after the full name in the same
  lesson. It is a *large granular lymphocyte* from the lymphoid progenitor, never a "small
  lymphocyte" — the script keeps the two on separate branches.
- **Fibrocyte and fibroblast (Chapter 9, not yet taught).** Freddo reverses the convention used
  by most textbooks: for him **fibrocyte** is the active cell and **fibroblast** the inactive
  one. Recorded here in advance because prior reading is the likeliest source of error when
  that chapter arrives.
