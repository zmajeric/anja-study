Neurons & glia

The neuron, myelin, synapses, five glia — and the retina.

Grey / white

Grey = cell bodies; white = myelinated axons. Brain: grey outside. Spinal cord: grey H inside. T1 MRI distinguishes them.

Neuron

Soma: large round euchromatic nucleus, one nucleolus; basophilic cytoplasm = Nissl bodies (rough ER; all protein machinery here). Axon: one or none (amacrine); output. Dendrites: many; inputs; spines; ≤1–2 mm. Typical big neurons: pyramidal, spinal motor, Purkinje (dendritic tree). EM axon: neurofilaments = clear areas; microtubules bundled in fuzzy actin.

Myelin

CNS oligodendrocyte (several axons) · PNS Schwann cell (one axon). Nodes of Ranvier: depolarisation only there → fast. Unmyelinated = slow but most peripheral sensory/autonomic fibres. PNS regenerates; CNS (optic nerve) does not.

Retina: ganglion cell axons unmyelinated in the retina (myelin is opaque), myelinated after the lamina cribrosa; nerve diameter ~1.5 mm → ~3 mm. Myelinated RNFL: congenital; absolute scotoma shaped like the patch; macula spared; stable; regresses if axons die (glaucoma).

Synapse, flow, end plate

Chemical: vesicles, voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels, re-uptake pumps, cleft, post-synaptic density; excitatory or inhibitory. Electrical: gap junctions (connexons). Axoplasmic flow: anterograde (slow mm/day; fast ~100×), retrograde. Motor end plate = NMJ; motor unit = all muscle cells of one neuron; folded post-synaptic membrane.
