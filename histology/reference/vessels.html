Pericytes vs smooth muscle

Pericytes: rounded nuclei bulging from the capillary wall (PAS trypsin digest); some contractile properties; lie within splits of the endothelial basement membrane. Smooth muscle cells: layered outside the basement membrane. Endothelial nuclei: elongated along the vessel.

Valves and the muscle pump

Valves = paired folds of intima; present below the heart, not above. Flow to heart flattens leaflets; reflux fills them so they meet. Soleus = "second heart": contraction squeezes the vein, upper valve opens; relaxation, lower valve closes and holds the column.

Clinical notes (Freddo's question sources)

  • Vasa vasorum / dissecting aneurysm: thick wall needs its own vessels; with age their lumen openings tear; blood forced through the intimal tear splits the media.
  • Posterior ciliary arteries = muscular arteries; central retinal artery (after first few mm) = arteriole.
  • Retinal AV crossing: shared tunica adventitia → arteriolar sclerosis thickens the arteriole → vein cannot slide away → focal constriction = AV nicking.

The heart

Endocardium ↔ intima · Myocardium ↔ media (striated cardiac muscle) · Pericardium ↔ adventitia: fibrous pericardium outside; serous pericardium = parietal layer (lines fibrous sac) + visceral layer (epicardium, on the myocardium); pericardial cavity between.
